# JobNova Product Marketing Challenge

Shikhar Sisodia | Santa Clara University

## Executive Summary

JobNova's strongest early-career message is not "AI will get you a job." It is: set up a better job-search workflow, discover relevant openings beyond a habitual feed, reduce repetitive application work, and keep human time for judgment, preparation, and relationships.

I recommend a utility-led LinkedIn post paired with a focused SCU distribution loop: LinkedIn content -> campaign landing page -> signup -> activation -> campus learning -> next content. The proposal treats JobNova feature and outcome statements as company claims unless independently verifiable.

## Product Understanding

JobNova's public site says it monitors 16,000+ company career pages, sends alerts, matches roles, customizes resumes, handles criteria-based auto-applications, and logs applications. Those are public product statements, not independently validated outcomes. The marketing message should promise controllable benefits: better coverage, faster discovery, relevance, and less repetitive work. It should not promise interviews, response rates, or employment.

| User pain | Applicable JobNova claim | Positioning guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Career-page monitoring | Do not imply every role appears there first. |
| Timing | Instant notifications | Do not imply a guaranteed advantage. |
| Application repetition | Criteria-based auto apply | Make candidate criteria and user-provided information clear. |
| Customization | Resume customizer | Do not guarantee ATS outcomes. |
| Signal and cognitive load | Matching and recommendations | Present as prioritization support, not certainty. |

## Task 1 - LinkedIn

### Why LinkedIn

LinkedIn is where early-career candidates already encounter career identity, job-search anxiety, and peer advice. The proposed content is designed to earn a save or comment before introducing JobNova.

### Target Audience

U.S. university students and recent graduates pursuing competitive technology or business internships and entry-level roles. They already use LinkedIn, Handshake, job boards, and company career pages. Their recurring frustrations are late discovery, repetitive forms, tailoring fatigue, fragmented tracking, and uncertainty about which openings are genuinely fresh.

- **Triggering event:** recruiting opens, a relevant role appears too late, or manual search begins crowding out study time.
- **Motivation:** direct effort toward opportunities that fit, while retaining time for projects, outreach, and interview preparation.
- **Objection:** automation could misrepresent the candidate or remove their judgment.
- **Conversion event:** a visitor starts a JobNova workflow and reaches a defined activation event.

### Comparable Post 1 - Michael Yan / Simplify

- **Post:** [POV: job searching in 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/myan_pov-job-searching-in-2026-ps-i-built-activity-7440515292029677568-16ry)
- **Format:** native image / meme with a short product note.
- **Public signal:** a visible public comment thread included candidates recognizing the entry-level search experience. No total-reaction claim is made here.
- **Analysis:** The POV framing creates immediate recognition. Humor makes a reply low-stakes, and the Simplify product note comes after the shared moment rather than before it. That sequence prevents the product from feeling like an interruption.
- **Lesson for JobNova:** begin with a recognizable pain, then introduce a clear workflow benefit.

### Comparable Post 2 - Adam Biddlecombe

- **Post:** [Most people spend months looking for a job](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/adam-bidd_most-people-spend-months-looking-for-a-job-activity-7201613287837368320-gpU1)
- **Format:** resource-list visual and text.
- **Public signal:** LinkedIn displayed 238 reactions and 81 comments when checked on August 13, 2026.
- **Analysis:** The list is organized by task, so readers can scan it, save it, or share it with a peer. The urgency in the hook earns attention, while the categorized utility creates the reason to stay and engage.
- **Lesson for JobNova:** provide useful, task-based information before a CTA. Do not copy the post's seven-day hiring promise.

### Comparable Post 3 - Amy Chick / Teal

- **Post:** [Unpopular opinion: I do not find Teal to be a helpful tool](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amychick_unpopular-opinion-i-dont-find-teal-to-be-activity-7176976601174167552-Ckk1)
- **Format:** text-led contrarian analysis.
- **Public signal:** LinkedIn displayed 125 comments when checked on August 13, 2026.
- **Analysis:** The contrarian hook is inherently commentable, but the post earns credibility through nuanced tradeoffs. Readers can respond with their own experience rather than simply agreeing with an endorsement.
- **Lesson for JobNova:** acknowledge that candidates still need judgment when using automation.

### Patterns I Would Apply

1. Start with a recognizable job-search moment.
2. Give the reader a useful routine before naming the product.
3. Use a conversational question that lets candidates share a specific pain.
4. Position automation as a way to reduce repetition, not a substitute for candidate judgment.

### Original JobNova LinkedIn Post

The complete copy is available in [`content/jobnova-linkedin-post.md`](./content/jobnova-linkedin-post.md).

> Most students are searching for internships in the same places, then wondering why every listing already feels crowded.
>
> That is not a reason to abandon LinkedIn, Handshake, or job boards. It is a reason to treat them as part of a system, not the whole system.
>
> A more useful weekly routine:
>
> - Define the roles, locations, and companies you actually want.
> - Watch company career pages for fresh openings.
> - Keep one clean source of truth for applications and deadlines.
> - Spend the saved time on the parts a tool cannot do for you: a strong portfolio, interview practice, and thoughtful outreach.
>
> The hard part of an early-career search is rarely a lack of effort. It is directing effort toward the right opportunities early enough.
>
> JobNova is built around that workflow: it says it monitors company career pages, matches roles to your criteria, and can handle repetitive application steps using the information you provide. The important part is still your judgment: set thoughtful criteria and review what represents you.
>
> If you are recruiting for internships or new-grad roles this year, what is the most repetitive part of your process?

### Pain Point and Why It Can Attract Users

The post addresses discovery, timing, repetition, and cognitive-load pain. The funnel is: impression -> pain recognition -> useful routine -> curiosity -> site visit -> signup -> activation. It should attract users because the advice is useful even without a click, while JobNova appears naturally as a possible way to operationalize the workflow.

The main failure point is a generic-advice outcome: candidates may engage with the routine but not see a product reason to visit. The UTM landing page must therefore connect company-page monitoring and repetitive-work reduction directly to the proposed routine.

### Expected Engagement and Measurement

This is an experiment hypothesis, not a forecast.

| Scenario | What it would look like | What it would mean |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | A small number of detailed comments | Learn the clearest pain language. |
| Base | Saves, peer tags, and questions about coverage or review boundaries | The routine has practical value. |
| Upside | A career creator or campus group reposts it | The content has value beyond the immediate network. |

| Metric | Hypothesis | Measurement | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified comments | Readers name discovery or repetition pain | Tag comments by pain type | Use recurring language in the next hook. |
| Saves and reposts | The routine is useful enough to revisit or share | Native LinkedIn analytics | Test a checklist or carousel variant. |
| Site visits to signup | The product bridge is clear | UTM link and product analytics | Clarify the landing page if visits do not activate. |
| Activation | Signups engage with a core workflow | Defined product event | Improve onboarding before expanding distribution. |

## Task 2 - In-Network Marketing Channels

These channels are accessible through Shikhar's university network. This proposal does not claim control, mailing-list access, partnership, attendance, or sponsorship rights.

### 1. SCU ACM - Partner - 9.0/10

- **Introduction:** ACM at Santa Clara University runs workshops, speakers, weekly meetings, and hackathons.
- **Target audience:** engineering, computer science, and AI-curious students.
- **Why relevant:** members closely match JobNova's early-career technology audience.
- **Activation:** offer a 25-minute job-search workflow workshop with an optional QR-code pilot.
- **Recommendation:** partner. The existing professional-development format makes a practical session more defensible than a promotional ask.
- **First next step:** ask whether an existing professional-development slot is available.
- **Evidence:** <https://www.scuacm.com/>

### 2. SCU Career Center - Partner - 8.5/10

- **Introduction:** the Career Center provides job-prep resources, coaching, Handshake access, and events.
- **Target audience:** students seeking internships, coaching, career fairs, and application support.
- **Why relevant:** it reaches students when search repetition and application management are most painful.
- **Activation:** propose a transparent pilot or session about reducing repetitive application work; position JobNova as a supplement to coaching and Handshake.
- **Recommendation:** partner. The upside is high, but institutional review is necessary.
- **First next step:** request a short discovery conversation and product demo.
- **Evidence:** <https://www.scu.edu/careercenter/>

### 3. Ciocca Center for Innovation and Entrepreneurship - Attend - 7.2/10

- **Introduction:** SCU describes Ciocca as a campus resource that activates the entrepreneurial mindset.
- **Target audience:** student founders and startup-minded students.
- **Why relevant:** founder-minded students may value early-stage role discovery and a structured search workflow.
- **Activation:** attend an open event first; if demand is clear, host a small office hour on early-stage role discovery and outreach tracking.
- **Recommendation:** attend. Validate the audience need before proposing programming.
- **First next step:** attend a public program and assess job-search demand.
- **Evidence:** <https://scholarcommons.scu.edu/cie/>

### 4. Hack for Humanity - Sponsor conditionally - 6.6/10

- **Introduction:** SCU describes Hack for Humanity as ACM's annual winter-quarter social-good hackathon.
- **Target audience:** technical students building portfolios and meeting peers and industry participants.
- **Why relevant:** participants are often preparing portfolios and considering internships or entry-level roles.
- **Activation:** sponsor only if the next announced edition permits a useful participant resource, such as a hiring-readiness mini-session with transparent attribution.
- **Recommendation:** sponsor conditionally. No future date, sponsor package, or access is assumed.
- **First next step:** request the next announced event or sponsor information before committing.
- **Evidence:** <https://www.scu.edu/engineering/stories/from-workshops-to-hackathons-acm-connects-students-through-computer-science.html>

## Recommended First Experiment

Over 30 days, publish the LinkedIn post with a UTM link, compare discovery and application-repetition hooks, run one approved SCU distribution moment, and compare activated signups by source. Continue only where qualified site visits convert to activation. Do not optimize for engagement alone.
