# JobNova Take-Home Walkthrough Script

Hi Kevin, thanks for taking the time to review my take-home. I am Shikhar Sisodia, and this is my product marketing and growth proposal for JobNova.

JobNova is an AI-driven job-search platform. Based on its public site, it says it monitors company career pages, sends alerts, matches opportunities, customizes resumes, and can automate repetitive application steps using a candidate's information. I was careful to treat those as JobNova product claims, not as independent proof of hiring outcomes.

The core user problem I focused on is job-search execution. For students and new grads, the search can become another part-time job: checking several feeds, finding a role after it feels crowded, submitting the same information repeatedly, and trying to tailor materials without losing time for projects, networking, and interview preparation. The message I would test is not that JobNova gets someone a job. It is that JobNova can help them spend less time operating the search itself.

I chose LinkedIn because it is where students and early-career candidates already talk about career pressure and share practical resources. I reviewed three public posts. The Simplify example shows how a recognizable job-search moment can earn attention before the product mention appears. The resource-list post by Adam Biddlecombe shows why task-based utility is saveable and shareable, though I would not copy its hiring-time promise. The Teal discussion by Amy Chick shows that nuanced tradeoffs can create substantive conversation. Together, those examples point to a post that recognizes the pain, gives useful advice, and makes the product connection naturally.

My proposed JobNova post begins with a simple observation: many students search in the same places and then wonder why listings already feel crowded. It gives a four-step weekly routine around criteria, career pages, organization, and spending saved time on high-value work. Only then does it explain JobNova's claimed role. The creative reinforces the idea without inventing statistics or using a fake LinkedIn interface.

For in-network distribution, I prioritized four SCU channels. First is ACM because of its engineering and computer-science audience and its existing workshops and hackathons. Second is the Career Center because it reaches students while they are actively preparing applications, though it will need a more careful partnership discussion. Third is the Ciocca Center for Innovation and Entrepreneurship, where I would first attend an open event and validate interest in early-stage career discovery. Fourth is Hack for Humanity, but only as a conditional sponsorship after the next edition and sponsor terms are announced. I do not assume access or partnerships with any of these organizations.

The first experiment is deliberately small. In week one, publish the LinkedIn post with a UTM link. In week two, compare discovery and repetition hooks. In week three, run one approved campus distribution moment. In week four, compare activated signups by source. The decision rule is to continue only where qualified visits turn into activation, rather than optimizing for likes alone.

Thanks again. I enjoyed thinking about how JobNova can earn attention with useful early-career job-search content and then turn that attention into a measurable growth loop.
